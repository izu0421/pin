Unmet need in CIDP

Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy is a rare autoimmune disorder in which the immune system attacks the myelin sheath of peripheral nerves — causing muscle weakness and sensory loss — and lacks curative or highly targeted therapies.

We combined single-cell and spatial multiomics on peripheral nerve biopsies and matched blood from CIDP patients and controls, and integrated the data with plasma proteomics, GWAS, and AI-based TCR modelling.

Three discoveries

  1. A repair signature in Schwann cells — stalled remyelination in the disease niche.
  2. Activated macrophages and T cells inside the nerve — CX3CR1⁺ M1 macrophages and CCL5⁺ CD8 T cells dominate the diseased microenvironment.
  3. Migratory inflammatory cells in blood — effector T/NK cells and IFN-activated monocytes exit the circulation via CX3CR1–fractalkine signalling.

Blocking CX3CR1-mediated immune infiltration could be a targeted therapy for CIDP — an alternative to the broad immunosuppression (IVIg) that is the current standard of care.

Anatomy of the peripheral nerve — profiled at cellular resolution

Xenium in situ sequencing of 477 genes and 27 proteins across control and CIDP sural nerve cross-sections, integrated with CellCharter to define spatial niches.

Cell types identified

Immune: Mast cells, macrophages (M1 CX3CR1⁺ ADAM28, M2 MNDA, M2 CLEC10A CCR2, M2 MS4A7, M2 LYVE1), T CD8 CCL5⁺, T CD4 CORO1A⁺ LCK⁺.

Stromal: Fibroblasts, adipocytes, mural, arterial and venous smooth muscle.

Nerve-support: Schwann cells — myelinating, non-myelinating (Remak bundles), repair Schwann / regenerating endoneurium, quiescent SOX2.

Vascular / barrier: Perineurium (blood-nerve barrier), venous endothelium, epineurium, endoneurium, pericyte microvasculature, epineurial perivascular / lymphatic.

Spatial niches (CellCharter)

Twelve reproducible spatial niches were defined, ranging from mature endoneurium and myelinating Schwann territory to a distinct regenerating endoneurium / repair Schwann niche and an immune-adipose aggregate that is enriched in disease.

% region occupied by the repair niche is elevated in CIDP relative to control, while the myelinating niche shrinks — the tissue-level footprint of failed regeneration.

Myelin morphology and Schwann cell repair — AI segmentation from Xenium autofluorescence

A fine-tuned CellPose-SAM + Gemma-aided CNN pipeline segments myelin rings directly from Xenium autofluorescence, letting us measure per-ring geometry and colocalise with immune cells at scale.

Repair-programme genes (APOD, JUN, EGR1) are elevated but myelin proteins (MPZ, PRX, PMP22, PLP1) are lost — a stalled repair state where Schwann cells commit to regeneration but never finish making compact myelin. Restarting the SOX10/EGR2 transcriptional programme is a candidate therapeutic strategy.

Contributors of inflammation in the nerve

Barrier dysfunction plus infiltrating inflammatory T cells and macrophages together define the diseased microenvironment.

Blood-nerve barrier integrity is impaired

SLC2A1 (GLUT1, a perineurium metabolic and barrier marker) is negatively correlated with disease enrichment in the nerve niches (r = −0.28, p < 0.0001). Loss of SLC2A1 tracks the same regions where inflammatory infiltrate accumulates — consistent with barrier breakdown enabling immune entry.

The macrophage subset expanded in the nerve carries the same fractalkine receptor CX3CR1 that marks the migrating monocytes and effector T cells in blood, pointing to a single axis (CX3CL1–CX3CR1) coordinating the shift from circulation to tissue.

Colocalisation panel (in poster): CX3CR1⁺ macrophages co-express CD11c, CD68, PLXDC2 and IRF8 — the phenotypic fingerprint of the inflammatory M1 subset within the nerve.

TCRfish — agentic AI + AlphaFold3 to find autoreactive T cells New method

Given a CIDP TCR repertoire, TCRfish orchestrates matching against reference disease-associated TCRs, infers common HLAs, predicts antigen presentation, and folds the peptide–MHC–TCR complex with AlphaFold3.

Pipeline

  1. Identify TCRs from CIDP samples (bulk + single-cell).
  2. Match against reference disease-associated TCRs (annotate by CDR3 and disease-enrichment).
  3. Infer common patient HLAs.
  4. Predict which myelin-derived peptides are presented.
  5. Fold the peptide-MHC-TCR complex with AlphaFold3 to score plausible binding.

Output

184 candidate peptides prioritised for experimental validation, spanning myelin target proteins MPZ, CNTN1, MBP, BDNF, MAG, PLP1, PMP2. Representative CDR3α/β pairs recovered from CIDP donors (from the poster):

alphagenome2niche — spatial context improves GWAS interpretation New method

A GNN-based framework that maps GWAS-significant SNPs onto spatial niches rather than isolated cell types — and outperforms cell-type-only prediction on held-out data.

Approach

  1. Learn gene-expression-based cell types and GNN-constructed spatial niches from Xenium data.
  2. Linear-probe on AlphaGenome to predict SNP-level effect on tissue.
  3. Feed CIDP GWAS-significant variants through the model to score effect on cell types and niches.

Summary — one axis, three compartments

Findings across compartments

  1. Blood. Effector T cells and IFN-activated monocytes exit the circulation via CX3CR1; TREM1⁺ inflammatory monocytes and immunosuppressive LDGs accumulate.
  2. Nerve. M1 CX3CR1⁺ ADAM28 macrophages colocalise with damaged myelin; CD8 CCL5⁺ T cells expand; barrier (SLC2A1) breaks down.
  3. Schwann cells. Repair programme is initiated but stalls — myelin proteins fall while APOD/JUN/EGR1 rise, and rings become hypertrophic rather than compact.

Blocking CX3CR1–fractalkine signalling could reduce immune infiltration of peripheral nerve tissue in CIDP. Combining this with a remyelination-promoting agent that restarts SOX10/EGR2 may give both anti-inflammatory and regenerative benefit.
